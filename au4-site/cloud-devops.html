Cloud & DevOps

Make change safer.
Make operations predictable.

Cloud and DevOps should reduce the uncertainty in delivery and operations — not add another layer of tools to maintain. We introduce only what genuinely earns its place.

Discuss your project →All services

What it’s for

Delivery speed is an architecture decision.

How quickly a team can release safely is usually decided by infrastructure, automation and feedback loops rather than effort. We look at the whole path from a commit to a running system, and remove the friction that costs the most.

New tools are easy to add and hard to remove. We introduce them only when the complexity earns its place.

In one line

Cloud & DevOps is not more tools.

It is making change safer and operations predictable — with only as much platform as the problem actually justifies.

When this work becomes relevant

Situations that usually bring people here.

These are patterns, not descriptions of past client work.

Releases are slow, manual or risky.

Environments differ unpredictably, so testing doesn’t predict production.

Infrastructure changes still depend on manual work.

Incidents are found by customers before monitoring finds them.

An application needs to scale more reliably.

Cloud spend grows faster than usage, and nobody can explain why.

Operational toil consumes engineering time.

Delivery and security controls are inconsistent across teams.

How AU4 approaches it

From commit to a system you can trust.

01

Map the delivery path

Understand how a change becomes a running system today — every manual and automated step in between.

02

Remove manual uncertainty

Find the fragile, inconsistent or undocumented steps that make releases tense.

03

Standardise what should repeat

Make infrastructure, environments and deployment behaviour reproducible rather than remembered.

04

Make change observable

Give teams the signals to know when something improved — or failed — without waiting for a customer.

05

Design for recovery

Assume failure is possible, and make the path back fast, practised and unremarkable.
